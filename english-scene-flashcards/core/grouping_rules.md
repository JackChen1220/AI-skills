# Scene Grouping Rules

## Goal
Group vocabulary into cards that can be understood through one coherent visual mechanism.

## Preferred grouping logic
A good group satisfies at least one:
1. same natural scene and simultaneously visible targets;
2. same action chain / micro-story;
3. same spatial or possession relation template;
4. same comparison/choice mechanism;
5. same timeline/context mechanism.

## Avoid
- grouping only because terms share a broad category;
- mixing concrete nouns with discourse/degree words without a special template;
- forcing abstract/function words into object-pointer cards;
- using a fixed 5-words-per-card rule when the scene becomes contrived.

## Card-size guidance
- preferred: 3–5 targets;
- 1–2 targets: allowed for difficult context/relation words;
- 6 targets: only if layout and semantic coherence remain strong;
- 7+ targets: normally split.

## Card-type routing
- `Object/Place`: concrete nouns, places, body parts.
- `Action`: visible verbs or event chains.
- `Relation`: near/into/top/side/possession relations.
- `Narrative`: lost/find/help/visit or other micro-stories.
- `Choice/Contrast`: or/right/wrong/change/difference.
- `Dialogue/Expression`: discourse markers, degree words, function words.
- `Timeline`: yesterday/later/month/time/soon.
- `Spatial/Cutaway`: rooms/regions/maps with dedicated layout rules.

## Review Gate
Score or qualitatively review:
- natural co-occurrence;
- visual distinctness;
- target mapping clarity;
- layout feasibility;
- connector feasibility;
- age appropriateness.

Weak groups must be split, retemplated or deferred before Scene Brief.
